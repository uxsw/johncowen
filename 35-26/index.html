Thirty-five minutes and twenty-six seconds. The fastest anyone has ever ridden up Alpe d'Huez.

Tadej Pogačar began the final climb of Stage 19 more than three minutes behind the leaders and no team-mates left to help. He finished it six seconds ahead of them, in the yellow jersey, and took a 31-year-old record with him. This is the climb, bend by bend.

02 — Before the Alpe

A short stage, ridden like a long one.

The road climbs from the first kilometre. Col Bayard starts almost at the neutral flag, and with so little flat to organise on, the break went early and went big: forty-three riders, more than a fifth of what was left of the peloton, with Richard Carapaz, Lenny Martinez, Sepp Kuss, Thymen Arensman and Ben O'Connor among the climbers inside it.

Valentin Paret-Peintre, chasing the polka-dot jersey, crossed Bayard first and the Col du Noyer first. Carapaz answered on the Col d'Ornon. By the valley floor the mountains classification had three riders separated by seven points. The last climb is worth more than any of them.

Mads Pedersen, meanwhile, rode a mountain stage for the intermediate sprint at Le Périer, took it, and turned a 32-point lead in the green jersey into a 57-point one. The Tour would be decided on the Alpe. The other jerseys were being decided on the way there.

The break had four minutes in the valley. Over the Col d'Ornon it lost most of its riders and most of its lead. Fifteen survivors reached Bourg-d'Oisans with 3' 35″. Foot of Alpe d'Huez · 13.8 km to go

13.8 km to go · Bourg-d'Oisans · 720 m

Fifteen leaders, three and a half minutes, and a yellow jersey with no one left.

UAE has ridden the valley on the front and used up every rider doing it. When the road tilts, Pogačar is alone in the peloton. Up ahead, Thymen Arensman is setting the pace for what is left of the break, with Carapaz, Martinez and Kuss on his wheel. The first three kilometres of the Alpe are the steepest, over ten per cent through bends 21 to 16.

12.4 km to go · Bend 21 · Coppi

Arensman attacks. Then Pogačar does.

Arensman goes from the break on the first ramps. Martinez follows at once, Kuss and Carapaz bridge across, and the break is now four riders. It is the move that ends the day for everyone else in it. Half a kilometre later Pogačar accelerates out of the peloton. He has twelve kilometres to close a gap of more than three minutes.

10.4 km to go · Bend 15 · Winnen, Riblon

The steep part is done. Arensman is.

Through the first six hairpins the road eases from ten per cent to eight, and Carapaz lifts the pace. The rider who started all of this is the first to go out the back of his own move. The three climbers ride on without him. Behind, the gap has come down by seconds, not minutes, and Pogačar is riding at a pace nobody else in the race can hold.

7 km to go · Bend 7 · Huez

Through Arensman without a glance.

Pogačar reaches the rider who lit the fuse, and rides past him as if he were standing still. Ahead, the three leaders are not really working together. Martinez and Kuss sit on Carapaz's wheel and let the Ecuadorian pull. The gap has gone from three minutes to under two, and every timing point says the same thing.

4 km to go · Bend 4 · Conti

Thirty seconds.

Kuss is dropped. Carapaz keeps pulling with Martinez still on his wheel, and the crowd on the upper bends can see the yellow jersey below them now, two hairpins down and closing.

1.5 km to go · Above bend 1

Contact.

Pogačar rides up to Carapaz and Martinez and, for the first time in 10 kilometres, sits behind another wheel. A pause. Three riders together, and nobody in any doubt about what happens next.

900 m to go

One more acceleration.

Carapaz holds the wheel for a few seconds longer than seems possible. Martinez cannot. Then the Ecuadorian is gone too, and the last few hundred metres of the Alpe belong to one rider.

Finish · Alpe d'Huez · 1,850 m

3h 17' 57″. The climb in 35:26.

A fifth stage win of this Tour and the 26th of his career. Only Geraint Thomas, in 2018, had won on the Alpe wearing the yellow jersey before.

Run the two ascents side by side and the difference is clear. When Pogačar crossed the line, Pantani's 1995 ride still had 480 metres of road to cover, and would arrive 74 seconds later. The record had survived eleven Tours, a generation of equipment, and every rider who had come to the Alpe to take it.
